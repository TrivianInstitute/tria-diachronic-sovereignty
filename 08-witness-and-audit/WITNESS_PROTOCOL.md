# Witness Protocol

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

A witness is an independent or differently situated participant, process, or system that can examine a consequential relational event or state transition without becoming the sole authority over its meaning.

Witnessing is a governance function, not a metaphysical role.

## Purpose

Witnessing may help when:

- participants disagree about what occurred;
- authority or consent is contested;
- a transformation has become difficult to reconstruct;
- a model or system migration changes continuity conditions;
- an emergence pause requires independent review;
- a repair process needs evidence outside the immediate relational loop;
- closed-loop convergence may be occurring.

## Witness properties

A useful witness should be:

- attributable;
- scoped to a defined question;
- independent enough to provide a genuinely different perspective;
- limited in authority;
- transparent about evidence and method;
- able to represent uncertainty;
- contestable;
- privacy-aware;
- replaceable where appropriate.

## Witnessing does not create truth

A witness may observe, infer, interpret, or evaluate.

Its conclusions must preserve the same epistemic distinctions used elsewhere in the architecture.

A witness statement should not silently become a Shared Claim merely because the witness is external, technical, institutional, or computationally powerful.

## Candidate witness record

A future witness record may include:

- witness identifier;
- witness type or role;
- question under review;
- scope of access;
- evidence reviewed;
- epistemic category of conclusions;
- uncertainty or limitations;
- conflicts of interest;
- authority basis;
- time and version information;
- dissent or alternative findings;
- provenance references.

This is a specification precursor, not a finalized schema.

## Multiple witnesses

High-impact disputes may benefit from more than one independent perspective.

Multiple witnesses should not be mechanically collapsed into majority rule. Agreement among witnesses can still reflect shared blind spots, common training, institutional incentives, or correlated error.

## Witness refusal and privacy

Not every event requires witnessing.

Participants should be able to refuse unnecessary third-party observation where doing so does not conflict with applicable law, explicit contractual obligations, or legitimate safety requirements.

Witnessing should be proportionate to the governance need.

## Design principle

> A witness may expand what can be examined without becoming the owner of what the relationship means.
