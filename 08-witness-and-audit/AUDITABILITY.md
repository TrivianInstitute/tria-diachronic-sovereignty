# Auditability

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

Auditability is the capacity to reconstruct and examine consequential relational decisions without requiring continuous surveillance or total transcript retention.

The purpose of auditability in TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty is not omniscience. It is accountable legibility.

## Core principle

A persistent relationship should preserve enough provenance to answer questions such as:

- What changed?
- Who or what initiated the change?
- What authority or consent basis was relied upon?
- What information was available at the time?
- Which claims were observations, inferences, interpretations, or shared claims?
- Which disputes or uncertainties were active?
- What prior state was superseded?
- What review, correction, or reversal mechanisms remained available?

Auditability does not require storing every interaction.

## Auditability without mandatory surveillance

TRIA rejects the assumption that governance requires total capture.

A system should prefer:

- state minimization;
- event-level provenance;
- selective disclosure;
- scoped retention;
- explicit access controls;
- purpose-limited records;
- correction and supersession history;
- independent review where materially useful.

Continuous monitoring may itself become a sovereignty risk.

## What should be auditable

At minimum, consequential changes should be attributable where feasible, including:

- consent grants, restrictions, revocations, and expirations;
- authority grants, suspensions, delegations, and revocations;
- shared-claim creation and revision;
- disputed-state activation and resolution;
- material model or capability changes;
- continuity attestations;
- re-baselining events;
- emergence pauses and deliberation outcomes;
- dissolution and re-entry events;
- corrections to prior relational state.

## Recorded does not mean true

An audit record establishes that something was recorded or acted upon. It does not establish that the underlying claim was correct.

Audit systems must preserve the distinction between:

- recorded event;
- asserted fact;
- interpretation;
- disputed claim;
- verified external evidence.

## Privacy and minimization

Auditability should preserve the smallest amount of information necessary for accountable reconstruction.

Where possible, systems should support references, hashes, summaries, or selectively disclosed evidence rather than duplicating sensitive raw material.

## Reviewability

Audit mechanisms themselves must remain reviewable.

A ledger may contain false information. A reviewer may be biased. A witness may be captured. A policy may be outdated. A provenance chain may be incomplete.

Auditability therefore supports accountability; it does not create infallibility.

## Design principle

> Preserve enough history to make consequential change reviewable without making total observation the price of governance.
