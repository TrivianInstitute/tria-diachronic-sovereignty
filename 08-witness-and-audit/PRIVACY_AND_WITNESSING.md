# Privacy and Witnessing

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

Witnessing and audit can improve accountability while also creating new risks of exposure, surveillance, coercion, and secondary use.

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty therefore treats privacy as a constraint on witnessing rather than an afterthought.

## Core principle

The existence of a governance need does not automatically justify maximal access.

Witnessing should be:

- purpose-limited;
- minimally scoped;
- proportionate to expected harm or consequence;
- access-controlled;
- attributable;
- time-bounded where feasible;
- explicit about retention and onward disclosure.

## Selective disclosure

A review process should prefer the least revealing evidence sufficient for the question.

Possible patterns include:

- summaries rather than raw transcripts;
- event metadata rather than full payloads;
- redacted evidence;
- scoped excerpts;
- cryptographic attestations;
- locally computed findings;
- privacy-preserving references.

These are implementation options rather than universal requirements.

## Private participant state

Participant-private state should not automatically become relational state merely because it may help resolve a dispute.

Examples include private memory, internal reasoning, personal records, biometric data, confidential organizational information, and other state not previously authorized for relational use.

Access to such material requires an independent authority or consent basis appropriate to the context.

## Surveillance risk

A governance architecture can become coercive if it treats continuous observation as the default condition of trust.

Persistent capture may:

- chill disagreement;
- inhibit experimentation or change;
- expose vulnerable participants;
- create institutional power asymmetries;
- make intimacy or participation conditional on surveillance;
- generate data that later exceeds its original purpose.

Auditability should therefore be designed around reconstructability, not total observability.

## Emergency and external authority

Some contexts may impose legal, safety, contractual, or institutional disclosure requirements.

Where these exist, the authority basis and scope should be represented explicitly. External authority should not be disguised as voluntary relational consent.

## Design principle

> Witness only what is necessary to make consequential governance reviewable, and do not confuse access with entitlement.
