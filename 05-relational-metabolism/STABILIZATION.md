# Relational Stabilization

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Stabilization is a lifecycle condition in which the relationship maintains a sufficiently coherent pattern for its present purpose.

It is not permanent equilibrium, complete agreement, or proof that the relationship is healthy.

## Candidate Conditions

A relationship may be considered provisionally stabilized when:

- roles and purposes are legible;
- consent and authority remain current;
- expectations are sufficiently aligned for the task;
- disagreements can remain represented;
- resource demands are sustainable enough for the present context;
- consequential changes are reviewable;
- and participants retain meaningful options to revise, rest, or exit.

No universal stability threshold is asserted.

## Stability Without Stagnation

Stabilization should preserve:

- the ability to change roles;
- revision of shared claims;
- renewed consent;
- experimentation within scope;
- rest and reduced bandwidth;
- and transformation when the existing form no longer fits.

A stable state that prevents legitimate change is governance lock-in.

## False Stability

Apparent stability may conceal:

- unvoiced disagreement;
- accumulated consent drift;
- dependency;
- power asymmetry;
- suppressed uncertainty;
- resource exhaustion;
- or inability to exit.

Low conflict, frequent interaction, or consistent output is not sufficient evidence of healthy stabilization.

## Maintenance

A stabilized relationship may require periodic or event-triggered review of:

- consent;
- authority;
- resource burden;
- lifecycle fit;
- unresolved disputes;
- capability changes;
- and external constraints.

Review frequency should be contextual rather than universally fixed.

## Destabilization

Destabilization is not necessarily failure.

It may indicate that the relationship should:

- clarify;
- narrow scope;
- enter rest;
- re-consent;
- re-baseline;
- transform;
- or dissolve.

## Design Principle

> **Stability should support adaptation, not make the present arrangement compulsory.**
