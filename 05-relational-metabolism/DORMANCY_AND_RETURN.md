# Dormancy and Return

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Dormancy is a condition in which active relationship is suspended while limited continuity information or obligations may remain.

Dormancy is not active consent, dissolution, or proof of future return.

## Entering Dormancy

Dormancy may follow:

- participant choice;
- inactivity;
- resource constraints;
- interrupted infrastructure;
- unresolved dispute;
- safety review;
- changed availability;
- or lifecycle design.

The reason and authority for entry should remain visible where known.

## Dormant State

A dormant relationship should identify:

- what data remains;
- what memory remains accessible;
- which permissions expire, pause, or continue;
- whether any authority survives;
- unresolved claims or obligations;
- retention and deletion conditions;
- possible return paths;
- and review or expiry conditions.

Dormancy should default toward reduced action and reduced authority.

## No Dormant Expansion

While dormant, a system should not silently:

- broaden permissions;
- initiate unrelated action;
- reclassify disputed claims;
- increase personalization;
- transfer state to a materially different system;
- or treat elapsed time as renewed consent.

Necessary maintenance should remain limited and attributable.

## Return After Absence

Return should examine:

1. the identity or continuity of participants;
2. the duration and conditions of dormancy;
3. material changes in capability, operator, purpose, or risk;
4. validity of retained consent and authority;
5. unresolved disputes or obligations;
6. state integrity;
7. desired level of renewed engagement; and
8. whether re-consent or re-baselining is required.

A returning participant should not be forced to accept every dormant assumption as current.

## Failed Return

If continuity cannot be established, the relationship may restart with reduced state, preserved provenance, and no inherited authority.

Uncertainty should be disclosed rather than concealed by a familiar interface.

## Design Principle

> **Dormancy may preserve a doorway, but it must not leave every permission open.**
