# Metabolic Transitions

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Metabolic transitions are governed movements between relational lifecycle states.

This document defines transition requirements, not a validated automated state machine.

## Candidate Transition Pattern

The architecture explores:

ENGAGE → DEEPEN → STABILIZE → REST → RENEW / TRANSFORM / DISSOLVE

DORMANT may be entered from several states, and return may lead to engagement, renewal, transformation, or dissolution.

Transitions need not be linear. Skipping states may be legitimate when the purpose and governance basis are clear.

## Transition Record

A consequential transition should record:

- relationship identifier;
- prior state;
- proposed or resulting state;
- initiating actor or event;
- stated purpose;
- consent basis;
- authority basis;
- resource implications;
- retained permissions and obligations;
- expired or suspended permissions;
- active disputes and uncertainty;
- provenance;
- effective time;
- reversibility;
- and review conditions.

## Transition Guards

Before a material transition, governance should ask:

- Is the initiating actor authorized?
- Is participant identity or continuity sufficiently established?
- Does existing consent cover the transition?
- Has relevant capability or risk changed?
- Are resource effects legible?
- Does the transition increase dependency or reduce exit?
- Are disputes being preserved?
- Is re-consent, re-baselining, or external review required?

## Invalid Silent Transitions

The architecture rejects automatic movement from:

- repeated interaction to broader consent;
- familiarity to trust;
- personalization to authority;
- silence to agreement;
- reliance to permanent retention;
- dormancy to active permission;
- stabilization to mandatory persistence;
- or renewed contact to restored authority.

## Concurrent and Partial Transitions

Different relational dimensions may transition separately.

Communication may renew while execution authority remains dormant. Memory may be retained for accountability while personalization is dissolved.

Partial transitions should be explicit rather than collapsed into one global status.

## Failed Transitions

A transition may fail because consent is absent, authority is insufficient, state is disputed, resources are unavailable, identity continuity is uncertain, or required safeguards cannot be met.

Failure should produce a safe, legible state rather than improvisational privilege.

## Future Specification

A machine-readable implementation may later define:

- enumerated states;
- allowed transition edges;
- required evidence;
- validation rules;
- event schemas;
- and failure responses.

Those details should remain traceable to the normative documents in this layer.

## Design Principle

> **No consequential lifecycle change should occur merely because continued interaction made it feel natural.**
