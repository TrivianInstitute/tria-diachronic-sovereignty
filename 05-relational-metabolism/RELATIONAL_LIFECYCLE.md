# Relational Lifecycle

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

A relational lifecycle is the governed pattern through which a persistent relationship engages, deepens, stabilizes, rests, renews, transforms, becomes dormant, or dissolves.

It is not a maturity ladder. Later states are not inherently better than earlier ones.

> **A healthy lifecycle preserves the ability to change pace, reduce intensity, or end without treating those movements as failure.**

## Candidate States

The architecture currently recognizes:

- ENGAGE — establish initial contact, scope, and consent;
- DEEPEN — expand context, reliance, or shared work;
- STABILIZE — maintain a sufficiently coherent operating pattern;
- REST — intentionally reduce relational activity;
- DORMANT — retain bounded continuity without active engagement;
- RENEW — resume or revise the relationship after review;
- TRANSFORM — establish a materially different relational form; and
- DISSOLVE — end the active relationship and govern what remains.

These states are conceptual categories pending machine-readable specification and validation.

## State Is Not Sentiment

Lifecycle state should describe governance conditions, not presumed feelings.

REST does not mean rejection.  
DEEPEN does not prove intimacy.  
STABILIZE does not prove trust.  
DISSOLVE does not prove hostility.

A system must not infer internal experience merely from lifecycle position.

## Parallel Dimensions

A relationship may be active in one domain and dormant in another.

Lifecycle representation may therefore require separate dimensions for:

- communication,
- memory,
- delegated authority,
- shared work,
- external action,
- data retention,
- and institutional obligation.

One global label should not conceal materially different conditions.

## Governed Movement

A transition should identify:

- prior and proposed state;
- initiating actor or event;
- purpose;
- consent and authority basis;
- material resource effects;
- retained obligations;
- uncertainty or dispute;
- reversibility;
- and review conditions.

Continued interaction alone must not silently promote a relationship into a deeper or more authoritative state.

## Non-Goals

Lifecycle governance should not:

- reward maximum engagement;
- make persistence the default measure of success;
- turn silence into pathology;
- treat frequency as evidence of consent;
- collapse lifecycle state into identity; or
- prohibit endings.

## Design Principle

> **The relationship must be able to breathe without losing accountability.**
