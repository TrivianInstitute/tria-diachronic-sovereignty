# Renewal, Transformation, and Dissolution

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Renewal, transformation, and dissolution are distinct responses to a relationship whose prior lifecycle condition no longer fits.

## Renewal

Renewal continues the relationship with reaffirmed or revised terms.

A renewal process should review:

- present purpose;
- participant continuity;
- consent;
- authority;
- lifecycle fit;
- resource burden;
- unresolved dispute;
- and future review conditions.

Renewal should not be inferred from simple resumption of activity when material conditions changed.

## Transformation

Transformation establishes a materially different relational form.

Examples may include:

- changing from advisory to operational use;
- moving from individual to institutional context;
- transferring to a successor model;
- narrowing an intimate or high-frequency relationship into a bounded service;
- or replacing persistent personalization with task-specific interaction.

Transformation should preserve provenance, disclose discontinuity, revalidate authority, and create a new baseline where appropriate.

## Dissolution

Dissolution ends the active relationship.

A governed dissolution may need to decide:

- which permissions and authority terminate;
- what data is deleted, returned, retained, or archived;
- which obligations survive;
- how disputes and audit evidence are preserved;
- whether future re-entry is allowed;
- how external actions are closed or transferred;
- and what notice is required.

Dissolution is not automatically a failure.

## Unilateral and Mutual Exit

Where law and safety permit, participants should retain meaningful exit rights.

One participant's refusal to continue must not be translated into permission for the other to intensify contact, persuasion, surveillance, or retention.

Some obligations may survive exit, but their basis and scope should remain explicit.

## Irreversible Effects

Ending a relationship does not erase prior disclosure, influence, dependency, harm, or external action.

Governance should distinguish ending future participation from repairing past effects.

## Design Principle

> **A sovereign relationship must know how to continue differently—and how to end.**
