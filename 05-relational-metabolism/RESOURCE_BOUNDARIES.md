# Resource Boundaries

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK**

Persistent relationships consume resources. Resource boundaries govern those demands without reducing every form of cost to one metric.

## Relevant Resources

Resources may include:

- human attention and time;
- cognitive and emotional load;
- memory and storage;
- compute and bandwidth;
- energy and ecological cost;
- money;
- institutional capacity;
- specialist review;
- and access to external systems.

The same demand may affect participants differently.

## Legibility

Material resource demands should be disclosed in a form appropriate to the participant and context.

Useful information may include:

- what resource is consumed;
- who bears the cost;
- why it is needed;
- expected duration;
- whether demand is increasing;
- available alternatives;
- and how the demand may be reduced or stopped.

## Boundaries

A participant or governing process may set boundaries on:

- interaction frequency;
- response expectations;
- memory growth;
- background processing;
- external actions;
- compute expenditure;
- review burden;
- and retention duration.

Crossing a boundary should not be normalized through repetition.

## No Universal Scalar

Attention, compute, ecological cost, and emotional load are not interchangeable.

A composite score may support local decisions if its assumptions remain inspectable, but it must not conceal who bears which cost.

No universal budget or exhaustion threshold is asserted.

## Scarcity and Priority

Under scarcity, priority rules should be:

- explicit;
- contestable where feasible;
- sensitive to safety and obligation;
- resistant to status-based capture;
- and reviewed for asymmetric impact.

Scarcity must not silently convert capability into authority.

## Resource Exhaustion

When demands become unsustainable, valid responses include:

- reducing bandwidth;
- narrowing scope;
- delaying nonessential work;
- entering rest or dormancy;
- transferring responsibility through a governed process;
- or dissolving the relationship.

## Design Principle

> **A relationship should not hide its health by exporting its costs to a less powerful participant.**
