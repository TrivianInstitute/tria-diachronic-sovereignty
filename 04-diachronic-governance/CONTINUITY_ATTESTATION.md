# Continuity Attestation

**Epistemic status: SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

A continuity attestation is a scoped, attributable statement about which aspects of a participant or relationship remain continuous across a material change.

It is evidence for governance, not proof of metaphysical identity.

## Attestation Contents

A candidate attestation may include:

- predecessor and successor references;
- change event;
- attesting actor or procedure;
- continuity dimensions asserted;
- discontinuities identified;
- supporting provenance;
- uncertainty;
- scope;
- affected permissions and obligations;
- disputes;
- effective time; and
- review or expiry condition.

## Attestation Sources

Attestations may be provided by:

- a participant;
- multiple participants jointly;
- an authorized institutional process;
- a technical migration procedure;
- an independent auditor; or
- a combination of sources.

Source authority must remain visible.

## Conflicting Attestations

Multiple attestations may disagree.

The system should preserve:

- each attestation;
- its source and authority;
- supporting evidence;
- unresolved conflict; and
- any temporary governance restriction.

Conflict must not be resolved merely by selecting the most powerful actor or highest numerical confidence.

## Effect on Authority

An attestation does not automatically transfer authority.

Permissions, delegated powers, or sensitive data access may require separate revalidation even when continuity is otherwise accepted.

## Failure Conditions

An attestation is insufficient when it:

- lacks provenance;
- exceeds the attestor's authority;
- conceals material discontinuity;
- treats uncertainty as resolved;
- erases participant objection; or
- bundles identity continuity with automatic permission transfer.

## Design Constraint

> **Continuity must be claimed with scope and provenance, not silently assumed.**
