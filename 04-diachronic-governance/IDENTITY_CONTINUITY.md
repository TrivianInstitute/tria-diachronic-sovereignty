# Identity Continuity

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK**

Identity continuity concerns whether a participant can be treated as meaningfully continuous across change without being imprisoned by an earlier representation.

The architecture does not define metaphysical personal identity or machine consciousness.

## Governance Question

> **Which earlier permissions, claims, obligations, and relationships remain applicable after material change, and why?**

## Continuity Dimensions

Continuity may involve different dimensions that should not be collapsed:

- legal or account identity;
- organizational role;
- model lineage;
- memory continuity;
- capability continuity;
- values or policy continuity;
- authority continuity;
- relational recognition; and
- provenance continuity.

Continuity in one dimension does not establish continuity in all others.

## Risks of Over-Continuity

Treating identity as too stable can produce:

- inherited permissions that no longer fit;
- stale preference models;
- permanent psychological classification;
- identity imprisonment;
- unreviewed authority succession; and
- denial of meaningful transformation.

## Risks of Under-Continuity

Treating every change as discontinuity can produce:

- erased accountability;
- broken obligations;
- consent laundering through replacement;
- loss of dispute history;
- repeated harm without lineage; and
- false claims of a clean slate.

## Continuity Review

A review should consider:

- what changed;
- which dimensions remain continuous;
- which claims are disputed or uncertain;
- what obligations should survive;
- what authority requires revalidation;
- whether participants recognize the successor relationship; and
- what cannot yet be determined.

## Representation

Continuity should be expressed as scoped, attributable claims rather than a single universal score.

## Design Principle

> **Preserve accountability without making provenance destiny.**
