# Relational Trajectory

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

A relational trajectory is the sequence and structure of consequential changes through which a relationship develops.

It is not equivalent to a transcript, activity log, or duration counter.

> **Trajectory governance asks whether participants can understand and redirect what the relationship is becoming.**

## Purpose

Single events may appear acceptable while their cumulative effect produces dependency, authority accumulation, narrowing options, or identity capture.

A trajectory representation should make cumulative change inspectable without requiring total memory.

## Candidate Elements

A trajectory record may reference:

- significant relational-state transitions;
- consent grants, renewals, restrictions, and revocations;
- changes in authority or capability;
- promoted, disputed, or expired claims;
- changes in lifecycle state;
- continuity reviews and re-baselines;
- unresolved harms or obligations; and
- provenance sufficient to explain each consequential transition.

## Temporal and Topological Views

Chronology records what happened when.

Relational topology records which earlier states remain structurally relevant now.

A later state may therefore reference an older consent boundary, unresolved dispute, or identity transition even when many ordinary interactions occurred between them.

## Material Change

A change may be materially relevant when it could alter a participant's reasonable understanding of:

- who or what is participating;
- what the system can do;
- what information is retained;
- what authority exists;
- what risks or dependencies are present; or
- whether earlier consent still applies.

No universal materiality threshold is asserted.

## Review Triggers

Candidate review triggers include:

- rapid authority expansion;
- model or operator replacement;
- significant capability change;
- accumulated consent drift;
- persistent unresolved dispute;
- major changes in data use or retention;
- movement into a higher-consequence domain; or
- a participant request for review.

## Non-Goals

Trajectory governance should not:

- reward perpetual engagement;
- treat greater intimacy as progress;
- require storage of full conversations;
- infer consent from continued use;
- freeze participants into earlier identities; or
- convert correlation into causal explanation.

## Design Constraint

> **Preserve enough history to govern change, but not so much that governance becomes surveillance.**
