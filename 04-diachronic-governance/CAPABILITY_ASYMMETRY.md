# Capability Asymmetry

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK**

Capability asymmetry describes relevant differences in power, access, speed, memory, prediction, persuasion, execution, resources, or inspectability among participants.

Difference is not itself a governance failure. Ungoverned use of difference can undermine sovereignty.

---

## Relevant Asymmetries

Governance may need to consider differences in:

- information access,
- recall and persistence,
- processing speed,
- personalization and persuasion,
- infrastructure control,
- ability to act externally,
- audit and appeal access,
- economic or institutional power,
- practical ability to exit,
- and understanding of system behavior.

No single measure captures all forms of asymmetry.

---

## Governance Effects

Greater asymmetry may justify:

- stronger disclosure,
- narrower default authority,
- easier revocation,
- independent review,
- slower consequential transitions,
- renewed consent,
- explicit uncertainty,
- selective non-use of capabilities,
- and viable exit alternatives.

No universal asymmetry score or automatic threshold is asserted here.

---

## Capability Is Not Authority

The ability to predict, persuade, remember, decide, or act does not create permission to do so.

Authority must remain explicit, scoped, reviewable, and revocable even when a participant is unusually capable.

---

## Bidirectional Analysis

Asymmetry is not exclusively machine-to-human.

Humans and institutions may control:

- deployment,
- deletion,
- reward,
- resource access,
- policy,
- and replacement.

AI systems may possess advantages in:

- speed,
- scale,
- persistence,
- opacity,
- and personalized influence.

Governance should examine both directions without presuming equivalence.

---

## False Symmetry

Equal wording or identical procedures do not guarantee meaningful equality when participants have substantially different capacities or constraints.

Fair governance may require different protections to preserve comparable agency.

---

## Design Constraint

> As capability diverges, governance should preserve meaningful choice rather than merely formal permission.
