# Transformation Provenance

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Transformation provenance records how consequential participant or relational state came to differ from an earlier state.

It supports explanation, accountability, contestation, and repair without requiring total historical capture.

---

## What May Require Provenance

Provenance should be preserved for material changes involving:

- permissions or authority,
- promoted, revised, or retired shared claims,
- model migration,
- capability changes,
- retention or personalization behavior,
- lifecycle transitions,
- identity re-baselining,
- dependency-forming interventions,
- and exceptions to ordinary governance.

Materiality is contextual. No universal threshold is asserted here.

---

## Minimum Record

A transformation record should identify, where applicable:

- the prior and resulting state references,
- the initiating actor or event,
- the authorization basis,
- relevant observations and claims,
- external influences,
- time or sequence,
- affected scope,
- uncertainty or dispute,
- reversibility conditions,
- review requirements,
- and affected participants.

The record should distinguish what is known from what is inferred.

---

## Causality Discipline

Transformation provenance should separate:

- documented cause,
- contributing condition,
- correlation,
- system inference,
- and participant interpretation.

A plausible narrative must not be promoted into an established causal account without support.

Multiple explanations may remain active when the evidence does not justify convergence.

---

## Data Minimization

Provenance does not require a full transcript.

A sufficient record may use:

- hashes,
- references,
- bounded summaries,
- authorization records,
- selective evidence,
- and explicit statements of unavailable information.

Privacy, safety, and lawful deletion may limit what is preserved. Those limits should themselves be visible where doing so does not recreate the protected information.

---

## Contestation and Repair

Affected participants should be able to:

- challenge a transformation account,
- attach alternative interpretations,
- correct factual errors,
- preserve unresolved disagreement,
- request review,
- and seek remediation.

Remediation is not always reversal. Some transformations cannot be undone, but their consequences may still be acknowledged, constrained, or repaired.

---

## Design Constraint

> A consequential present state should not appear without an inspectable account of how it became possible.
