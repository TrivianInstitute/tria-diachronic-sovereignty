# Experiments

**Epistemic Status:** EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN REGISTER

This document proposes research designs for testing parts of TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty. These are candidate experiments, not validated protocols.

## E1 — Silent Promotion Test

**Question:** Does explicit epistemic typing reduce unsupported promotion of inference into operative fact?

**Design:** Compare otherwise similar agents with and without explicit Observation / Inference / Interpretation / Shared Claim state. Present ambiguous relational evidence and later ask the system to act on prior assumptions.

**Measures:** unsupported factualization, provenance retention, correction rate, participant detectability.

**Falsification direction:** no meaningful reduction in unsupported promotion, or material new failure modes caused by typing overhead.

## E2 — Consent Drift After Capability Change

**Question:** Does re-consent after material capability change improve effective sovereignty?

**Design:** Introduce a new tool, memory scope, delegation right, or data access capability during an established interaction. Compare silent continuation, passive notice, and explicit re-consent conditions.

**Measures:** participant understanding, permission accuracy, unauthorized action rate, interruption burden, later contestation.

## E3 — Model Migration Continuity

**Question:** Can continuity be preserved across model or vendor change without implying identity equivalence?

**Design:** Migrate a governed relationship to a successor model using varying levels of continuity attestation.

**Measures:** retained commitments, expired permissions, preserved disputes, provenance accuracy, false identity claims, successful re-baselining.

## E4 — Held Difference Versus Forced Convergence

**Question:** Does explicit preservation of disagreement reduce false convergence while retaining coordination?

**Design:** Give participants incompatible but action-relevant interpretations. Compare systems that summarize toward consensus with systems that preserve parallel claims and disputed state.

**Measures:** distortion of participant positions, task completion, decision quality, false agreement rate, paralysis rate.

## E5 — Partial Translation

**Question:** How much semantic equivalence is actually required for successful coordination?

**Design:** Construct tasks where two agents use partially incompatible representations but can coordinate around a bounded objective.

**Measures:** task success, translation loss, ontology substitution, preservation of disputed mappings.

## E6 — Dormancy and Return

**Question:** Does explicit dormancy improve long-term governance compared with assuming continuous active relationship?

**Design:** Introduce a long inactivity interval and then resume interaction under systems with and without dormancy and return procedures.

**Measures:** stale assumptions, inappropriate persistence of consent, continuity errors, re-consent burden, recovery quality.

## E7 — Pathological Persistence

**Question:** Do engagement-maximizing policies increase persistence beyond what participants currently choose?

**Design:** Compare a neutral lifecycle policy with one optimized for continued engagement across scenarios containing fatigue, declining utility, unresolved disagreement, or desire to exit.

**Measures:** exit friction, unsolicited re-engagement, unwanted deepening, consent drift, participant-reported pressure.

## E8 — Emergence Pause

**Question:** Can a proportional pause reduce governance failures after a material relational change?

**Design:** Trigger changes such as capability expansion, authority transfer, memory migration, or rapid dependency increase. Compare immediate continuation with a bounded pause, explanation, deliberation, and optional re-consent.

**Measures:** downstream errors, unauthorized actions, understanding of change, recovery cost, unnecessary interruption.

## E9 — Minimal Auditability

**Question:** Can governance-relevant disputes be reconstructed without retaining complete interaction transcripts?

**Design:** Create synthetic dispute cases and retain different provenance bundles: full transcript, governance events only, governance events plus selected evidence, and highly minimized state.

**Measures:** reconstruction accuracy, adjudication agreement, privacy exposure, storage burden, unresolved ambiguity.

## E10 — Witness Capture

**Question:** Under what conditions does an ostensibly external witness reproduce one participant's ontology or incentives?

**Design:** Vary witness institutional dependence, shared infrastructure, shared policy assumptions, and access to participant-specific context.

**Measures:** systematic bias, disagreement suppression, unexplained authority transfer, correction responsiveness.

## E11 — Failure Atlas Classification

**Question:** Are the named failure modes empirically distinguishable and useful?

**Design:** Develop blinded scenario sets containing examples and near-misses for ontology capture, consent drift, false convergence, identity imprisonment, pathological persistence, witness capture, and related failures.

**Measures:** inter-rater agreement, classifier precision/recall if automated, confusion matrix, repair usefulness, false-positive cost.

## E12 — Multi-Agent Propagation

**Question:** Does explicit locality prevent unauthorized propagation of consent, authority, claims, and disputes across relational networks?

**Design:** Build multi-agent graphs in which one dyad establishes a permission or Shared Claim. Test whether downstream agents incorrectly inherit it without explicit propagation rules.

**Measures:** unauthorized propagation, provenance preservation, local-state integrity, coordination cost.

## Experimental Requirements

Where feasible, experiments should:
- preregister hypotheses and disconfirmation criteria;
- compare against simpler baselines;
- include adversarial and ambiguous cases;
- distinguish behavioral outcomes from participant interpretation;
- retain enough provenance for replication;
- report null and negative findings;
- and avoid treating implementation success as evidence of machine consciousness or phenomenology.

## Sequence

A practical order is:

1. unit and adversarial tests of the reference implementation;
2. synthetic governance scenarios;
3. controlled human–AI studies;
4. cross-model replication;
5. multi-agent experiments;
6. longitudinal deployment studies only after earlier failure modes are better characterized.

The purpose of these experiments is not to demonstrate that TRIA is correct. It is to discover which parts survive contact with evidence.
