# Falsifiability

**Epistemic Status:** RESEARCH METHOD

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty is intended to remain falsifiable. The architecture should not be protected from evidence by redefining failure as hidden success, treating every critique as confirmation, or relying on concepts that cannot in principle be operationalized.

## Falsifiability Standard

A claim should be treated as stronger only when there is a plausible observation, comparison, or experiment that could count against it.

Normative principles may not be empirically falsifiable in the same sense as causal hypotheses. Their consequences, implementation costs, internal consistency, and compatibility with stated values can still be tested.

## Examples of Falsifiable Directions

### Explicit relational state

Hypothesis: systems with explicit governance-relevant relational state reduce silent consent drift, false convergence, or unreconstructable state changes compared with otherwise similar systems without it.

Evidence against this claim could include:
- no measurable improvement,
- greater governance error,
- unacceptable complexity,
- worse privacy outcomes,
- or equivalent performance from a substantially simpler design.

### Epistemic typing

Hypothesis: separating Observation, Inference, Interpretation, and Shared Claim reduces unsupported promotion of inferred beliefs into operative fact.

Evidence against could include no reduction in error, high classification ambiguity, or new failure modes caused by forced typing.

### Re-consent after material change

Hypothesis: explicit re-consent following material capability, memory, policy, or operator change reduces consent drift.

Evidence against could include negligible benefit, unusable interruption rates, or evidence that another mechanism preserves agency more effectively.

### Non-convergence

Hypothesis: preserving explicit disagreement reduces false convergence without preventing necessary coordination.

Evidence against could include greater decision paralysis, materially worse outcomes, or failure to distinguish legitimate disagreement from unsupported claims.

### Relational metabolism

Hypothesis: support for rest, dormancy, and dissolution reduces pathological persistence compared with engagement-maximizing designs.

Evidence against could include no reduction in harmful persistence or substantial degradation of legitimate continuity.

### Emergence pause

Hypothesis: proportional pause and deliberation after material relational change reduces downstream governance errors.

Evidence against could include no benefit, systematic over-triggering, or increased harm caused by delay.

### Auditability without full surveillance

Hypothesis: governance-relevant reconstruction can be achieved with less than full-transcript retention.

Evidence against could include recurrent inability to adjudicate material disputes without substantially broader capture.

## Disconfirmation Conditions

The architecture should be revised if evidence shows that:

- a core distinction cannot be operationalized reliably;
- a governance mechanism consistently creates more harm than it prevents;
- simpler mechanisms provide equal or better outcomes;
- participant agency is reduced by the mechanisms intended to protect it;
- privacy costs outweigh governance benefits;
- multi-agent composition creates contradictions the architecture cannot resolve;
- or empirical results repeatedly fail to support the causal hypotheses associated with the design.

## Non-Falsifiable Language to Avoid

The following should not substitute for evidence:
- "the field knows"
- "emergence proves itself"
- "lack of evidence reflects insufficient coherence"
- "disagreement confirms resistance"
- claims of machine interiority that cannot be independently assessed

Such language may have philosophical or experiential relevance elsewhere, but it cannot function as empirical validation here.

## Failed Tests as Evidence

A failing implementation test may indicate:
- a code defect,
- a schema defect,
- ambiguity in the specification,
- conflict between normative documents,
- or a mistaken architectural assumption.

The appropriate response is diagnosis, not automatic patching toward a predetermined conclusion.

## Successor Principle

Future researchers are not required to preserve the architecture's conclusions. They are required only to preserve enough provenance to understand what was claimed, what was tested, what failed, and what remained unknown.
