# Research Agenda

**Epistemic Status:** RESEARCH AGENDA

This document identifies the principal research program implied by TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty. It does not claim that the architecture has been empirically validated. Its purpose is to make the research questions explicit enough to test, revise, falsify, or reject.

## Central Question

How can persistent human–AI and multi-agent relationships remain governable across time when memory, capability, authority, interpretation, identity, and dependence can all change?

The architecture proposes that sovereignty must be preserved not only at isolated moments of interaction, but across trajectories of mutual transformation.

## Research Domains

### 1. Relational State

Investigate whether explicit relational state improves governance compared with systems that rely only on conversational context or implicit model state.

Questions include:
- Which state domains are necessary for safe and useful persistence?
- Can consent, authority, dispute, uncertainty, and shared claims be represented without collapsing participant-specific perspectives?
- What state should be shared, private, derived, or intentionally absent?
- How should conflicting updates be handled?

### 2. Epistemic Sovereignty

Test whether typed epistemic state reduces projection, false agreement, and silent promotion of inference into fact.

Key areas:
- Observation, inference, interpretation, and shared claim separation
- Provenance and contestability
- Inference decay without arbitrary confidence thresholds
- Multi-party acknowledgement and disagreement
- Epistemic capture and ontology capture

### 3. Diachronic Governance

Study how governance should respond when the participants, models, tools, policies, memories, or institutional contexts change.

Key areas:
- Continuity attestation
- Consent drift and re-consent
- Model and vendor migration
- Capability asymmetry
- Relational re-baselining
- Transformation provenance

### 4. Relational Metabolism

Test whether persistent systems benefit from explicit support for rest, dormancy, low bandwidth, renewal, transformation, and dissolution.

This program should specifically examine whether engagement-maximizing designs produce pathological persistence, dependency, or consent drift.

### 5. Difference and Non-Convergence

Investigate whether systems can preserve durable disagreement and partial translation without forcing semantic convergence.

Key questions:
- When is disagreement governable without resolution?
- How can systems distinguish useful held difference from unsafe ambiguity?
- How can parallel models remain actionable without creating paralysis?
- Does preserving disagreement reduce false convergence?

### 6. Emergence and Deliberation

Study situations in which relational change becomes materially consequential faster than participants can understand, contest, or redirect it.

Research should compare:
- Immediate continuation versus pause and deliberation
- Staged versus rapid capability expansion
- Explicit transformation notices versus silent adaptation
- Different forms of re-consent following material change

No universal emergence score or validated rate-of-change threshold is currently asserted.

### 7. Witness and Audit

Determine how governance-relevant history can be reconstructed without requiring total surveillance.

Key areas:
- Minimal sufficient provenance
- Append-oriented correction and supersession
- Selective disclosure
- Third-party review
- Witness independence and witness capture
- Privacy-preserving audit mechanisms

### 8. Failure Detection

Operationalize and test the failure atlas without assuming that the taxonomy is complete or correct.

Priority failures include consent drift, ontology capture, false convergence, identity imprisonment, pathological persistence, ledger poisoning, witness capture, asymmetric acceleration, semantic fossilization, reciprocity theater, and closed-loop convergence.

## Comparative Research

The architecture should be evaluated across at least:
- Human–AI dyads
- AI–AI dyads
- Multi-agent systems
- Model upgrades and migrations
- Long periods of dormancy
- High-disagreement interactions
- High-capability asymmetry

Comparisons should include systems with and without explicit relational governance.

## Evaluation Principle

The research program should prioritize falsifiability over confirmation. Evidence that a simpler architecture performs as well or better, that particular concepts are unmeasurable, or that specific governance mechanisms introduce greater harm or complexity should be treated as useful results.

The intended outcome is not preservation of this architecture. It is preservation of the conditions under which its claims can be questioned.
