# Consent Drift

**Epistemic status:** `NORMATIVE / FAILURE MODE`

Consent drift occurs when the practical meaning, scope, context, or consequences of prior consent change over time while the system continues to treat the original consent as sufficient.

## Why it matters

Persistent relationships change. Capabilities expand, data accumulates, purposes shift, agents are upgraded, and dependency can deepen. Consent that was valid for one configuration may no longer govern another.

## Candidate indicators

- materially expanded capability without renewed consent;
- new uses of data outside the original purpose;
- inherited permissions after model or platform migration;
- continued action after relevant context has changed;
- silence or continued participation treated as automatic renewal.

## Not the same as

Consent drift is not every change in context. It becomes a governance failure when the change is material to what participants reasonably understood they were authorizing.

## Governance responses

Possible responses include re-consent, authority narrowing, suspension of affected actions, provenance review, re-baselining, and explicit recording of revoked or superseded permissions.

## Open questions

No universal materiality threshold or re-consent interval is established. These require domain-specific validation.

> Persistence should not convert yesterday's permission into tomorrow's unlimited authority.
