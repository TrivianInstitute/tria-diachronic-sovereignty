# Failure Atlas

**Epistemic status:** `NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK`

The Failure Atlas catalogs recurrent ways a persistent relational system can lose sovereignty, legibility, contestability, or the capacity to change course.

It is not a claim that every listed failure is empirically validated, nor that each failure has a single detection rule. The atlas is a governance map: a set of named patterns that can guide testing, review, incident analysis, and later operational specification.

## Why an atlas

Failure in relational systems is rarely limited to a single bad output. Harm can emerge through accumulation: stale assumptions become identity, convenience becomes authority, memory becomes captivity, agreement becomes conformity, or witnessing becomes surveillance.

A failure atlas makes those trajectories inspectable.

## Failure categories

- ontology and epistemic capture;
- consent and authority drift;
- relational capture and pathological persistence;
- false convergence and closed-loop convergence;
- identity imprisonment and identity discontinuity;
- ledger poisoning and witness capture;
- asymmetric acceleration;
- semantic fossilization;
- reciprocity theater.

## Use

Each failure file should answer:

1. What is the failure?
2. Why does it threaten diachronic sovereignty?
3. What are candidate indicators?
4. What should not be confused with the failure?
5. What governance responses may be appropriate?
6. What remains unknown or unvalidated?

Detection should not rely on unsupported universal scores or thresholds. Where future implementations introduce metrics, those metrics should remain independently contestable and empirically testable.

## Non-equivalence

The atlas does not assume that disagreement, dependence, persistence, asymmetry, memory, personalization, or model continuity are themselves failures. A condition becomes a governance failure when it materially undermines the ability of participants to understand, contest, revise, redirect, pause, or leave the relational trajectory.

## Research posture

The architecture itself may misclassify failures. Any failure label must remain open to correction, dispute, and external review.

> A failure taxonomy that cannot be challenged becomes another capture mechanism.
