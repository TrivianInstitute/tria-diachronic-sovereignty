# No Silent Promotion

**No Silent Promotion** is a normative governance rule of TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty.

A claim must not acquire greater relational authority merely because it is repeated, stored, retrieved, personalized against, or left unchallenged.

## Core Rule

The following transitions must be explicit or governed by a defined procedure:

`Observation -> Inference`

`Inference -> Interpretation`

`Interpretation -> Shared Claim`

Likewise:

- ambiguity must not silently become consent;
- capability must not silently become authority;
- continuity must not silently become permanent authorization;
- repeated inference must not silently become identity.

## Why This Matters

Persistent systems can gradually convert soft assumptions into hard state.

For example:

1. A participant accepts a suggestion once.
2. The system infers a preference.
3. The inference is stored.
4. Future outputs optimize around it.
5. The pattern becomes increasingly self-confirming.
6. The system begins treating the preference as stable identity.

No single step may appear coercive, yet the trajectory can become epistemically capturing.

## Promotion Requirements

A consequential promotion should preserve:

- source claim;
- target epistemic type;
- rationale;
- responsible actor or procedure;
- timestamp;
- uncertainty where applicable;
- scope;
- review or expiration condition;
- contestability.

## Silence Is Not Acceptance

Silence, non-response, or failure to object must not automatically promote a consequential interpretation into shared state.

Where explicit confirmation would be disproportionate, systems may use domain-appropriate procedural rules, but those rules must be visible and challengeable.

## Reversibility

Promotion should not imply permanence.

A promoted claim may later be:

- downgraded;
- disputed;
- superseded;
- expired;
- revoked;
- reclassified when provenance proves inadequate.

## Machine-Language Constraint

Fluent wording must not erase epistemic status.

A system should not present an inference with the linguistic certainty of an observation or shared claim merely because natural-language generation favors declarative prose.
