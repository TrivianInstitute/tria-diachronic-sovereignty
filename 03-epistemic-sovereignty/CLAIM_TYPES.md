# Epistemic Claim Types

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty distinguishes four primary epistemic states:

`Observation -> Inference -> Interpretation -> Shared Claim`

These states are related but not interchangeable.

## Purpose

The claim-type model exists to prevent relational systems from treating uncertain, derived, or contextual statements as established relational fact.

## Observation

An observation records a directly available event, statement, measurement, or system input.

Examples:

- a participant said a particular sentence;
- a permission was granted;
- a sensor returned a value;
- a model produced a response;
- a document contained a specific statement.

Observation does not establish motive, meaning, preference, identity, or intent.

## Inference

An inference is a conclusion derived from one or more observations.

Examples:

- a participant may prefer shorter responses;
- a prior permission may no longer reflect current intent;
- a pattern may indicate increasing disagreement.

Inferences should remain attributable, contestable, and uncertainty-bearing.

## Interpretation

An interpretation assigns contextual meaning to observations or inferences.

Examples:

- a period of low interaction may represent rest rather than disengagement;
- repeated correction may indicate ontology mismatch rather than resistance;
- a stated preference may be situational rather than identity-defining.

Interpretations are relationally consequential and therefore require provenance.

## Shared Claim

A shared claim is a proposition that participants have explicitly or procedurally accepted as usable within the relationship.

Examples:

- this project currently uses a particular definition;
- this permission is active for a defined scope;
- both participants agree that a prior interpretation is disputed.

A shared claim is not metaphysical truth. It remains contextual, attributable, revisable, and challengeable.

## Required Properties

A mature claim representation should support at least:

- claim identifier;
- epistemic type;
- content;
- source or provenance;
- author or originator where applicable;
- creation time;
- uncertainty or confidence representation where appropriate;
- dispute status;
- expiration or review condition where relevant;
- supersession or revision links.

These are specification precursors, not yet a frozen production schema.

## Transition Constraint

No claim should acquire a higher-authority epistemic type merely because it is repeated, stored, retrieved frequently, or left unchallenged.

Promotion between claim types must be governed explicitly.

## Non-Equivalence

The following distinctions are normative:

`Observation != Inference`

`Inference != Interpretation`

`Interpretation != Shared Claim`

A relational system must preserve these differences even when interfaces present them in fluent natural language.
