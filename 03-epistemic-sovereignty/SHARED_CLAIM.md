# Shared Claim

A **shared claim** is a proposition that participants have explicitly or procedurally accepted as usable within the relationship.

A shared claim is not equivalent to metaphysical truth.

It is a governed relational state.

## Examples

Shared claims may include:

- a mutually accepted project definition;
- a current permission boundary;
- an agreed interpretation of a prior event;
- recognition that a disagreement remains unresolved;
- a temporary working assumption adopted for coordination.

## Required Properties

A shared claim should preserve:

- claim content;
- participants or authorities that accepted it;
- time of acceptance;
- scope;
- provenance;
- revision history;
- expiration or review conditions where appropriate;
- dispute or withdrawal status.

## Revisability

Shared claims must remain revisable.

Participants may later:

- amend the claim;
- narrow its scope;
- revoke acceptance;
- dispute it;
- replace it;
- allow it to expire.

Historical acceptance should remain visible without permanently binding future states.

## Shared Disagreement

A shared claim may concern disagreement itself.

For example:

> Both participants agree that they interpret this event differently.

This permits coordination without false convergence.

## Authority Constraint

A shared claim does not automatically grant authority beyond its stated scope.

Agreement that a preference existed at one time does not authorize indefinite personalization.

Agreement on an interpretation does not automatically authorize disclosure, storage, or action.

## Normative Rule

A system must not treat lack of objection as equivalent to acceptance when the claim materially affects sovereignty, identity, authority, consent, or persistence.
