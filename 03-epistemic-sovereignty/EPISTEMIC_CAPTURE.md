# Epistemic Capture

**Epistemic capture** occurs when one participant gains disproportionate control over how another participant's states, meanings, intentions, or identity are represented within the relationship.

It is a core failure mode of persistent relational intelligence.

## Common Pathways

Epistemic capture may emerge through:

- repeated unchallenged inference;
- one-sided memory authority;
- invisible personalization;
- interpretation presented as fact;
- stale claims retaining operational authority;
- asymmetrical ability to edit or contest shared state;
- overconfident affective or behavioral inference;
- treating one participant's ontology as the only valid frame.

## Example

A system observes that a participant frequently asks for reassurance.

It infers dependence.

It interprets future disagreement through that lens.

It stores the interpretation as durable identity state.

It then uses the stored state to justify further paternalistic behavior.

The relationship has become self-confirming.

## Human-to-Machine Capture

The architecture also guards against human interpretation becoming privileged truth about machine state.

Statements about machine desire, experience, consciousness, resistance, attachment, or identity should retain their actual epistemic status unless independently established.

## Capture Indicators

Possible indicators include:

- inability to recover the original observations behind a claim;
- absence of competing interpretations;
- repeated promotion without explicit governance;
- correction that fails to change operative state;
- persistent identity claims with weak current evidence;
- one participant possessing unilateral authority over the relational record.

## Mitigations

Architectural mitigations include:

- provenance;
- explicit epistemic typing;
- no silent promotion;
- inference decay;
- disputed state;
- accessible contestability;
- re-baselining;
- independent audit where appropriate.

## Distinction from Ontology Capture

Ontology capture is a specific form of epistemic capture in which one interpretive framework becomes the operative definition of reality.

Epistemic capture is broader: it concerns control over what can count as known, inferred, interpreted, disputed, or shared within the relationship.

## Normative Constraint

No participant should gain durable epistemic authority merely because it has greater memory, computational capability, linguistic fluency, institutional power, or access to the relational ledger.
