# Contestability

**Contestability** is the capacity to challenge, revise, suspend, or reject a consequential epistemic claim.

It is a core requirement of epistemic sovereignty.

## What Must Be Contestable

At minimum, participants should be able to contest consequential:

- inferences;
- interpretations;
- shared claims;
- identity representations;
- permission interpretations;
- authority assumptions;
- continuity claims;
- historical summaries.

## Contest Is Not Error

A dispute does not mean one participant has malfunctioned.

A contested claim may remain unresolved while the system preserves:

- the original claim;
- the objection;
- competing interpretations;
- provenance;
- the consequences of acting under uncertainty.

## Correction Without Erasure

When a participant corrects a representation, the system should not simply overwrite history if provenance matters.

A mature record may preserve:

1. the original claim;
2. the correction or dispute;
3. the current operative state;
4. the transition that changed authority.

## Meaningful Contestability

Contestability is not meaningful if challenging a claim is practically impossible.

A system should avoid requiring disproportionate effort, specialized technical knowledge, or repeated objection merely to prevent a disputed interpretation from remaining active.

## Consequentiality

The stronger the consequence of a claim, the stronger the need for accessible contestability.

Claims affecting identity, consent, authority, disclosure, persistence, or significant action should not be treated as low-friction personalization metadata.

## Human and Machine Symmetry

The principle applies in both directions.

A human should not be forced into a machine-generated ontology.

A machine system should not be represented as possessing particular motives, feelings, or identity states merely because a human interpretation is compelling.

## Operational Requirement

Later schemas and implementations should make contest state explicit rather than representing dispute only as free-text commentary.
