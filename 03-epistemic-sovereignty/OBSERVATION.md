# Observation

An **observation** is the lowest-assumption epistemic state in TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty.

It records what was directly available to the system without silently adding explanation, motive, identity, or meaning.

## Examples

Observation may include:

- a participant's explicit statement;
- a logged permission change;
- a recorded action;
- a model output;
- a sensor reading;
- a document value;
- a timestamped system event.

## Observation Is Not Meaning

The observation:

> The participant did not respond for seven days.

does not establish:

- disinterest;
- distress;
- withdrawal of consent;
- hostility;
- desire for continued contact;
- any particular psychological state.

Those are possible inferences or interpretations.

## Provenance

An observation should preserve enough provenance to answer:

- what was observed;
- by which system or participant;
- when;
- through what channel or instrument;
- whether the record is complete or partial;
- whether the observation itself is disputed.

## Fallibility

Observations can still be wrong.

Sensors fail. Logs can be incomplete. Speech can be transcribed incorrectly. Context can be omitted. A participant may dispute whether an event was represented accurately.

Therefore, **observation does not mean infallible fact**.

It means that the claim is represented as directly sourced rather than derived.

## Governance Rule

An observation may support inference or interpretation, but it must not silently become either.

The original observation should remain recoverable when a consequential downstream claim depends on it.
