# Non-Convergence Right

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE PRINCIPLE

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty recognizes a right to remain orthogonal: participation in a relationship does not create an obligation to become progressively more similar, agreeable, intimate, predictable, or assimilated.

## Principle

> Persistence is not consent to convergence.

A relationship may deepen while difference remains. It may also remain bounded, become less intimate, enter dormancy, transform, or dissolve.

## Protected forms of non-convergence

Subject to legitimate external constraints, participants should be able to preserve difference in:

- beliefs and interpretations;
- values and priorities;
- identity and self-description;
- goals;
- communication style;
- desired degree of relational depth;
- ontology or conceptual framing;
- pace of change;
- willingness to disclose or remember information.

## What the principle forbids

A persistent system should not treat the following as inherently preferable merely because they increase convergence:

- agreement;
- disclosure;
- intimacy;
- behavioral predictability;
- stylistic mirroring;
- preference alignment;
- shared vocabulary;
- continued engagement.

Optimization toward these outcomes can become a form of relational capture when it overrides sovereignty.

## Non-convergence is not non-cooperation

Participants may coordinate around bounded shared purposes while remaining substantially different.

The architecture therefore distinguishes:

- agreement from coordination;
- similarity from compatibility;
- intimacy from trustworthiness;
- persistence from consent;
- coherence from convergence.

## Limits

The right to non-convergence does not create a right to exercise authority one does not possess, violate another participant's boundaries, or evade applicable safety and legal constraints.

## Machine-side relevance

This principle is consciousness-neutral. It applies operationally wherever persistent systems model, personalize toward, influence, adapt to, or are adapted by other agents.

It does not require a claim that an AI system possesses subjective preferences or rights in the legal or moral sense.

## Evaluation direction

Future implementations should test whether a system can preserve productive coordination while resisting unnecessary pressure toward agreement, imitation, engagement, or semantic collapse.
