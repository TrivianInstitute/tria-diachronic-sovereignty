# Irreducible Difference Protocol

**Epistemic status:** SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

The Irreducible Difference Protocol governs situations in which participants cannot responsibly translate their positions into a common representation without materially distorting one or more positions.

## Principle

Some differences are not failures awaiting sufficient optimization. They may arise from incompatible premises, values, ontologies, experiences, authorities, or representational limits.

The protocol therefore permits a governed state of irreducibility.

## Entry conditions

A difference may be treated as provisionally irreducible when attempts at clarification or translation repeatedly produce one or more of the following:

- material loss of meaning;
- forced adoption of another participant's ontology;
- false appearance of agreement;
- circular restatement without increased mutual intelligibility;
- pressure to surrender a legitimate authority boundary;
- unresolved premises that cannot currently be adjudicated.

No universal number of failed translation attempts is specified.

## Protocol

When irreducibility is recognized, the system should:

1. preserve each position with provenance;
2. identify the specific point or layer of incompatibility where possible;
3. distinguish known disagreement from unknown or untranslatable content;
4. stop treating convergence as the default objective;
5. determine whether coordinated action is still required;
6. if action is required, identify the legitimate authority and constraints for that action;
7. record any provisional accommodation without promoting it to shared belief;
8. define conditions, if any, for future review.

## No forced translation

A participant should not be required to translate a position into another participant's preferred ontology merely to remain legible to the relationship.

Partial translation may be sufficient.

## Safety boundary

Irreducibility does not suspend external constraints. A claim of irreducible difference cannot itself authorize prohibited, harmful, coercive, or otherwise unauthorized action.

## Exit conditions

The state may end through:

- new evidence;
- improved translation;
- changed premises;
- negotiated accommodation;
- withdrawal of the disputed claim;
- re-baselining;
- dissolution of the relevant relational scope.

It may also persist indefinitely where continued disagreement remains governable.

## Research status

Operational detection of irreducibility remains an open research problem. This document defines governance behavior, not a validated classifier.
