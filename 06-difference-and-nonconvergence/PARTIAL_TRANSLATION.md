# Partial Translation

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE PRINCIPLE + SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

Partial translation allows participants to establish enough shared intelligibility for a bounded purpose without claiming complete semantic equivalence.

## Core principle

> Successful coordination does not require total mutual translation.

A translation may preserve only the portion of meaning necessary for a particular decision, action, or relational boundary.

## Why partial translation matters

Persistent intelligent systems can create pressure toward semantic compression: one vocabulary, one ontology, one canonical interpretation. That can improve efficiency while also erasing meaningful distinctions.

Partial translation offers a different pattern: preserve what can be responsibly mapped and mark what remains unmapped.

## Translation record

A governed partial translation should be able to represent:

- source expression or claim reference;
- source participant;
- target representation;
- purpose or scope of the translation;
- known preserved meaning;
- known loss, ambiguity, or mismatch;
- disputed mappings;
- translator or transformation provenance;
- review or expiration conditions where relevant.

These fields are specification precursors rather than a final schema.

## Non-equivalence

A usable translation must not silently imply identity between source and target representations.

For example:

`A maps sufficiently to B for purpose P`

is not equivalent to:

`A = B`.

## Authority

Translation does not transfer authority. A system's ability to paraphrase, classify, or map a participant's position does not authorize the system to redefine that position.

Where the participant contests a translation, the contestation should remain attached to the translation state.

## Failure modes

Partial translation fails when:

- approximation is stored as equivalence;
- omitted meaning disappears from provenance;
- the target ontology becomes mandatory;
- a translation optimized for one purpose is reused outside its scope without review;
- system-generated interpretation is attributed to the source participant.

## Research questions

Future work should test how partial translations can remain useful across long-running systems without accumulating semantic distortion or excessive representational overhead.
