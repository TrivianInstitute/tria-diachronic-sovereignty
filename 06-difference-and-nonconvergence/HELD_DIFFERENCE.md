# Held Difference

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE PRINCIPLE + SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

Held difference is the capacity of a relationship to preserve meaningful disagreement without requiring immediate resolution, assimilation, withdrawal, or domination.

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty treats difference as potentially durable relational state rather than automatically as error.

## Core principle

> A persistent relationship may remain coherent without becoming convergent.

Participants may disagree about interpretation, values, priorities, causal models, identity, meaning, or desired outcomes while continuing to relate under mutually intelligible governance.

Held difference therefore protects both relationship and orthogonality.

## Requirements

A held difference should be:

- attributable to the participant or source that holds it;
- distinguishable from observation and shared claim;
- represented without silently selecting a winner;
- contestable by affected participants;
- reviewable when new evidence or changed conditions matter;
- permitted to remain unresolved when resolution is not necessary for legitimate action.

## Difference is not symmetry

Preserving disagreement does not imply that all claims have equal evidentiary support, moral standing, or authority.

A system may represent that one position is better supported while still preserving another participant's disagreement. Held difference must not become a mechanism for laundering falsehood, abuse, coercion, or unauthorized action into equivalence.

## Governance relevance

When a difference materially affects consent, authority, safety, continuity, or action, the system should surface the disagreement rather than collapse it into a single relational belief.

Where action must occur despite unresolved difference, the decision should preserve:

- the competing positions;
- the authority basis for the action;
- the uncertainty or dispute;
- the decision provenance;
- the conditions under which the decision may be revisited.

## Relationship to Shared Claim

A Shared Claim is not produced merely because participants continue interacting.

Repeated exposure, familiarity, silence, lack of objection, or model confidence must not silently transform held difference into agreement.

## Failure conditions

Held difference fails when:

- disagreement is erased for convenience;
- one participant's interpretation is stored as relational fact;
- continued participation is treated as agreement;
- pressure toward harmony suppresses legitimate dissent;
- disagreement is preserved performatively while downstream behavior ignores it.

## Open questions

Research remains necessary on how systems can preserve durable disagreement without producing unusable state, false balance, or decision paralysis.
