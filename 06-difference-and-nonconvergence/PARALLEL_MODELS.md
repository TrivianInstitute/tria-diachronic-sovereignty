# Parallel Models

**Epistemic status:** SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

Parallel Models permit a relational system to retain multiple non-identical representations of the same event, condition, participant, or trajectory when premature collapse into one model would destroy relevant information.

## Principle

A persistent relationship does not always need one canonical interpretation.

Where models differ materially, the system may preserve them in parallel while recording their provenance, scope, evidence, authority, and downstream consequences.

## Examples

Parallel models may be appropriate when:

- two participants interpret the same interaction differently;
- an observed behavior supports multiple plausible inferences;
- a model upgrade changes interpretation of historical state;
- institutional and participant accounts differ;
- two ontologies provide useful but non-equivalent descriptions;
- uncertainty cannot yet be responsibly resolved.

## Requirements

Each model should remain distinguishable by:

- source or author;
- epistemic type;
- supporting evidence;
- scope;
- creation and revision provenance;
- known uncertainty;
- disputes or objections;
- authority for any action derived from it.

## Action under parallel models

Parallel representation does not eliminate the need to act.

If a decision is required, the system should record which model or authority basis informed the action and why, while preserving the alternatives where governance relevance remains.

The selected action model must not silently become the relationally shared model.

## Consolidation

Models may later be reconciled, superseded, narrowed, or retired. Consolidation should be explicit and provenance-preserving.

Historical models should not remain operational merely because they once existed; continued authority requires present relevance.

## Resource constraint

Preserving every possible model indefinitely would be impractical and could itself undermine governance. Retention should therefore be proportional to governance relevance, not maximal memory.

## Failure modes

- premature canonicalization;
- false equivalence among models with radically different support;
- hidden selection of one model for downstream behavior;
- indefinite retention of obsolete models;
- multiplication of models used to avoid legitimate decisions.

No universal model-retention count or scoring rule is asserted.
