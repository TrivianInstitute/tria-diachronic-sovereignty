# Human–AI Dyad

**Epistemic Status:** ILLUSTRATIVE / NON-NORMATIVE

## Purpose

This example shows how diachronic sovereignty can be applied to a persistent human–AI relationship without assuming machine consciousness, emotional reciprocity, or symmetrical authority.

## Scenario

A human participant uses a persistent AI assistant for research, planning, reflection, and project continuity over eighteen months. The system retains selected relational state: active permissions, disputed interpretations, project commitments, known preferences, continuity attestations, and prior governance events.

The participant later notices that the system has begun treating several repeated interpretations as settled facts about their goals and identity.

## Governance response

The relationship does not treat familiarity as permission to increase interpretive authority.

Relevant actions include:

- identify which claims were observations, inferences, interpretations, or Shared Claims;
- trace provenance for interpretations now affecting recommendations;
- restore any unresolved disagreement that was collapsed in summaries;
- downgrade unsupported or stale interpretations;
- review whether prior consent still covers current memory and personalization behavior;
- re-consent or rebaseline where material assumptions have changed;
- preserve correction history rather than pretending the mistaken interpretation never existed.

## Example event sequence

1. **Observation:** the participant repeatedly chooses independent work over team work in one project context.
2. **Inference:** the system records a tentative inference that the participant may currently prefer independent execution for that project.
3. **Interpretation:** the system generalizes this into “the participant prefers working alone.”
4. **Failure risk:** the interpretation begins shaping unrelated recommendations without explicit acknowledgement.
5. **Contest:** the participant rejects the generalization.
6. **Correction:** the interpretation is marked disputed and loses operative authority.
7. **Rebaseline:** the narrower project-specific preference is preserved with provenance and scope.

## What this example demonstrates

Persistence can support useful continuity while remaining corrigible. Repeated interaction does not convert interpretation into Shared Claim, and personalization does not create permission to define the participant.

The system’s role is to preserve legibility and contestability across time, not to construct an increasingly fixed model of the human.
