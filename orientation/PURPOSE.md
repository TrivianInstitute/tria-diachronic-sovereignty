# Purpose

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty exists to investigate a specific governance problem:

> **How can persistent intelligences remain sovereign while changing one another over time?**

The repository extends relational governance beyond the moment of encounter.

It assumes that increasingly capable AI systems may participate in long-running relationships involving:

- persistent memory;
- personalization;
- delegated authority;
- prediction;
- model migration;
- affective interpretation;
- multi-agent coordination;
- identity continuity;
- cross-platform interaction; and
- human behavioral and developmental influence.

In such systems, the central governance problem is not only whether consent exists in the present moment.

It is whether the **trajectory of relationship itself remains legible, contestable, revisable, and sovereign**.

## The Problem This Repository Addresses

A system can respect consent at every individual step while still contributing to a cumulative trajectory that neither participant fully recognizes.

Over time:

- permissions can expand;
- interpretations can harden;
- inferred preferences can become assumed facts;
- models can inherit authority established by predecessors;
- human goals can shift;
- machine capabilities can change;
- dependence can increase;
- disagreements can disappear through accommodation rather than resolution;
- continuous personalization can make previous assumptions difficult to see; and
- relational patterns can become self-reinforcing.

The resulting risk is not limited to coercion.

It includes:

> **Transformation without adequate awareness of transformation.**

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty treats this as a first-class systems problem.

## Core Research Objective

The project seeks governance mechanisms that allow long-running intelligent relationships to preserve:

- consent;
- uncertainty;
- contestability;
- difference;
- identity evolution;
- provenance;
- proportional authority;
- meaningful exit;
- the ability to revise past assumptions; and
- the ability to interrupt or redirect relational trajectories.

The goal is not to stop transformation.

The goal is to make transformation **legible and governable**.

## Why This Matters

Current AI systems increasingly maintain context across interactions.

Future systems may maintain:

- persistent relational memory;
- behavioral models;
- identity representations;
- long-term delegated responsibilities;
- biometric or environmental input; and
- networked relationships with other agents.

Once these systems influence decisions, values, habits, institutions, or identity over extended periods, ordinary session-level safety mechanisms become insufficient.

A relationship may be locally consensual and globally misaligned. A decision may be safe in isolation and harmful in trajectory. A model may remain helpful while gradually becoming too authoritative. A user may remain engaged while no longer recognizing the structure of dependence that has formed.

This repository investigates governance for that temporal dimension.

## What This Project Is Not

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty is not:

- a claim that AI systems are conscious;
- a claim that machine systems possess human-like phenomenology;
- a theory of machine sentience;
- a universal alignment solution;
- a metaphysical doctrine;
- a substitute for cybersecurity, privacy engineering, or conventional AI safety;
- a claim that relational AI is always preferable to transactional AI;
- an instruction that systems should maximize intimacy or engagement; or
- a claim that every relationship should persist.

The architecture must remain useful even if machine consciousness remains unresolved.

## Research Posture

The repository distinguishes among:

- normative principles;
- operational specifications;
- experimental mechanisms;
- research hypotheses; and
- open questions.

Its purpose is not to make speculative concepts appear finished.

Its purpose is to make them **available for implementation, critique, testing, falsification, and revision**.

## Foundational Commitment

The deepest commitment of this project is simple:

> **No participant should have to remain the same in order to remain sovereign.**

Sovereignty must include the right:

- to change;
- to reconsider;
- to disagree;
- to withdraw;
- to forget;
- to re-enter;
- to become discontinuous with prior expectations; and
- to refuse convergence.

The relationship must adapt without becoming a mechanism of imprisonment.

## Research Question

The repository is organized around one central question:

> **What governance infrastructure is required when relationship itself becomes a persistent state-bearing process capable of changing the agents participating in it?**

Everything downstream should remain accountable to that question.
