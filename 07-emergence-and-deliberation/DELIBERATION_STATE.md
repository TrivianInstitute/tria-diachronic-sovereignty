# Deliberation State

**Epistemic status:** SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

A deliberation state is a governed interval in which consequential relational questions are made explicit before further high-impact action proceeds.

Deliberation is not a requirement for every interaction. It is appropriate when unresolved change may affect sovereignty, consent, authority, continuity, identity representation, resources, or exit conditions.

## Deliberation object

A future implementation should be able to represent at least:

- `deliberation_id`;
- affected relationship or group relationship;
- initiating event or concern;
- participants and relevant decision-makers;
- contested or uncertain claims;
- affected consent and authority references;
- material capability or context changes;
- available options;
- constraints and non-negotiable boundaries;
- evidence and provenance references;
- unresolved differences;
- review or expiry conditions;
- outcome and resulting state-transition references.

This field set is a specification precursor, not a final schema.

## Deliberation principles

Deliberation should preserve:

- attributable positions;
- epistemic category distinctions;
- uncertainty;
- legitimate non-convergence;
- the ability to revise or withdraw a position;
- asymmetries in technical capability, information, or institutional power;
- privacy and access boundaries;
- the possibility that no consensus is reached.

## Consensus is not required

A deliberation process does not succeed only when participants agree.

Valid outcomes may include:

- bounded agreement;
- partial agreement;
- parallel models;
- a preserved dispute;
- narrower permissions;
- external review;
- re-consent;
- re-baselining;
- continued pause;
- dissolution.

## Decision authority

The ability to participate in deliberation is distinct from authority to make a final decision.

Where decision authority is asymmetric or externally constrained, the architecture should make that boundary explicit rather than simulating equal authority.

## No coercive deliberation

Deliberation must not become a mechanism for wearing down refusal, manufacturing consent, or demanding semantic agreement as the price of continuation.

Repeated questioning after a clear refusal can itself become a governance failure.

## Record and provenance

Consequential deliberation outcomes should be attributable and linked to the relational ledger without requiring storage of a complete private transcript.

The record should distinguish:

- what was proposed;
- what was observed;
- what was inferred;
- what was interpreted;
- what was agreed as a Shared Claim;
- what remained disputed or unknown;
- what authority or consent changed as a result.

## Design principle

> Deliberation should make consequential change governable without making agreement compulsory.
