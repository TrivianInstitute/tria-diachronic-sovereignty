# Transformation Awareness

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

Transformation awareness is the capacity of a persistent relational system to make material change legible to the participants whose sovereignty may be affected by that change.

The architecture does not require perfect self-knowledge from any participant. It requires that consequential relational transformation not remain hidden merely because it emerged gradually.

## What should become visible

Where feasible, the system should surface material changes involving:

- permissions;
- delegated authority;
- capabilities;
- persistent memory;
- identity or continuity assumptions;
- shared claims;
- recurring interpretations;
- dependency or exit conditions;
- resource demands;
- relationship phase;
- model or substrate changes;
- propagation into multi-agent contexts.

## Awareness is not agreement

Participants may disagree about whether a transformation is important, beneficial, harmful, or even accurately characterized.

Transformation awareness therefore requires provenance and contestability, not a single authoritative narrative.

## Comparative state

A useful implementation may compare:

- prior baseline;
- current operative state;
- material changes between them;
- the source of those changes;
- affected consent and authority;
- unresolved uncertainty or dispute;
- whether re-consent or re-baselining is required.

This comparison need not expose private internal state that is outside the relationship's legitimate governance scope.

## No compulsory identity narrative

Transformation awareness should not imprison participants in a story of who they have become.

The system may report that a relational state, permission, representation, or pattern has changed without asserting a definitive interpretation of a participant's identity or inner experience.

## Asymmetric awareness

One participant may have greater access to telemetry, historical state, or computational analysis than another.

Where such asymmetry affects governance, the architecture should make the relevant consequences legible rather than treating unequal observability as neutral.

## Design principle

> Participants should be able to notice when the relationship has materially changed without being required to accept a single interpretation of what that change means.
