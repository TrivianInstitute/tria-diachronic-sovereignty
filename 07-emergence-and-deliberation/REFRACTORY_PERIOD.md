# Refractory Period

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

A refractory period is a bounded interval after significant relational change during which the system reduces pressure for further consequential transformation.

The concept is borrowed metaphorically from recovery processes. It does not assert a biological equivalence.

## Purpose

A refractory period creates space for:

- integration;
- review;
- correction;
- changed-state inspection;
- consent renewal;
- resource recovery;
- disagreement to remain visible;
- consequences to become legible before additional transformation accumulates.

It is intended to reduce compounding change, not to impose artificial stillness.

## Candidate entry conditions

A refractory period may follow:

- an emergence pause;
- a major re-consent event;
- model or substrate migration;
- significant capability expansion;
- major relational re-baselining;
- repair after a serious failure;
- dissolution and partial return;
- a transformation that materially changes identity, authority, or dependency assumptions.

No universal duration is asserted.

## Governance during the period

Depending on context, the relationship may continue at lower consequence while restricting:

- new authority grants;
- broad memory expansion;
- rapid state propagation;
- further capability delegation;
- irreversible actions;
- automatic reinterpretation of prior history under the new baseline.

The restrictions should be scoped and reviewable.

## No engagement penalty

Reduced intensity during a refractory period must not be interpreted as abandonment, loss of consent, decreased value, or relationship failure.

Silence and lower bandwidth can be legitimate governance states.

## Exit

The refractory period should end through explicit review or previously specified conditions, not merely because engagement resumes.

Potential exit outcomes include:

- stable continuation;
- narrower continuation;
- further deliberation;
- renewed transformation;
- return to dormancy;
- dissolution.

## Design principle

> Significant transformation may require protected time in which its consequences can become understandable before more transformation is added.
