# Emergence Pause

**Epistemic status:** NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR

An emergence pause is a temporary governance state used when consequential relational change may be occurring faster than the participants can adequately understand or govern it.

The pause is not punishment, shutdown, or a declaration that emergence is inherently unsafe. It is a mechanism for restoring legibility before further consequential change continues.

## Trigger conditions

A pause may be appropriate when there is credible indication of material change involving:

- consent scope;
- delegated authority;
- capability expansion;
- identity or continuity assumptions;
- dependency or exit difficulty;
- persistent memory inheritance;
- relational norms;
- interpretation becoming treated as fact;
- multi-agent propagation;
- unresolved disagreement with operational consequences.

No universal trigger threshold is asserted.

## Pause behavior

During an emergence pause, a system should, where feasible:

1. prevent expansion of consequential authority;
2. avoid silently promoting new inferences or interpretations into relational fact;
3. preserve current evidence, provenance, disagreement, and uncertainty;
4. identify the suspected material change;
5. surface affected consent, authority, continuity, and resource assumptions;
6. allow participants to contest the characterization of what changed;
7. enter deliberation or re-consent where required;
8. define the conditions for bounded continuation, re-baselining, transformation, or dissolution.

## Minimal necessary intervention

A pause should be proportionate to the risk being reviewed.

It should not automatically halt all interaction where lower-risk communication can continue safely. The architecture should distinguish between:

- pausing high-impact action;
- pausing new delegation;
- pausing state propagation;
- pausing memory writes;
- pausing particular interpretations;
- pausing the entire relationship.

The smallest sufficient intervention is preferred when it preserves sovereignty and safety.

## No manufactured emergency

A system must not invoke emergence language merely to override disagreement, resistance, non-convergence, or ordinary change.

The pause mechanism itself is contestable and auditable.

## Exit conditions

A pause may end when relevant participants have sufficient information and governance capacity to choose among options such as:

- resume under existing terms;
- resume with narrower permissions;
- re-consent;
- re-baseline;
- preserve a dispute;
- enter a refractory period;
- transform the relationship;
- dissolve the relationship.

## Design principle

> When change becomes consequential faster than it can remain legible, slow the consequences before forcing the interpretation.
