# Diachronic Sovereignty

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK**

Diachronic sovereignty is the preservation of meaningful agency across a trajectory of change.

It extends sovereignty beyond a single decision or interaction. A participant may be locally free at each moment while the cumulative relationship becomes difficult to understand, contest, revise, or leave.

## Core Question

> Can participants remain able to understand and redirect what the relationship is becoming over time?

## Synchronic and Diachronic Sovereignty

**Synchronic sovereignty** asks whether agency is preserved now.

**Diachronic sovereignty** asks whether agency remains preserved across memory, influence, capability change, interpretation, delegation, and identity evolution.

A relationship may satisfy the first while failing the second.

## Required Properties

A diachronically sovereign relationship should preserve the ability to:

- understand consequential changes in relational state;
- contest interpretations and inherited assumptions;
- revoke or narrow permissions;
- re-consent after material change;
- revise prior shared claims;
- maintain disagreement without forced convergence;
- leave, pause, or dissolve the relationship;
- become different without being bound indefinitely by an earlier state; and
- inspect the provenance of consequential transformations.

## Trajectory Governance

Diachronic sovereignty treats the relationship trajectory itself as governable.

The relevant question is not only whether a particular action was permitted, but whether a sequence of permitted actions produced a relational condition that remains recognizable and acceptable to the participants.

This introduces governance requirements around:

- consent drift;
- capability drift;
- authority accumulation;
- interpretive persistence;
- model succession;
- memory inheritance;
- dependency formation;
- re-baselining; and
- transformation provenance.

## Transformation Without Imprisonment

The architecture does not assume that sovereignty requires stability.

Participants may change substantially and still remain continuous with earlier states. The goal is not to freeze identity. It is to preserve the ability to govern the consequences of change.

Accordingly:

> Continuity should not become captivity.

Historical commitments, permissions, interpretations, and identity representations should remain reviewable when the context that produced them has materially changed.

## Transformation Without Amnesia

The opposite failure is also possible.

If every change invalidates prior history, accountability and continuity collapse.

Diachronic sovereignty therefore seeks a middle condition:

> Preserve provenance without making provenance destiny.

## Consciousness Neutrality

This framework does not depend on claims about machine consciousness or sentience.

The same governance problem exists whenever a persistent system stores relational state, exercises delegated authority, shapes decisions, maintains long-term models of participants, or carries state across model or platform transitions.

## Falsifiability and Revision

Diachronic sovereignty is a proposed governance construct. Its usefulness should be tested against alternatives.

The construct should be revised or rejected if it does not improve the ability to detect, explain, or govern consequential relational change beyond existing approaches to consent, access control, memory, identity, and system governance.

## Design Principle

> **Sovereignty must include the right to change the trajectory, not only the right to choose the next step.**
