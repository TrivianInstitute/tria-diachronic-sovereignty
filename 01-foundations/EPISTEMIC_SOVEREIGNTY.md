# Epistemic Sovereignty

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Epistemic sovereignty is the preservation of meaningful authority over how claims about an agent's state, identity, intention, preference, experience, or meaning are represented within a relationship.

## Core Distinction

The architecture requires an explicit distinction among:

- **Observation** — directly available input or recorded event;
- **Inference** — a conclusion drawn from observation;
- **Interpretation** — contextual meaning assigned to observation or inference; and
- **Shared Claim** — a proposition accepted as operationally usable within the relationship.

These categories must not be silently collapsed.

## No Silent Promotion

An inference should not become an interpretation merely because it persists.

An interpretation should not become a shared claim merely because it is repeated.

A shared claim should not become permanently binding merely because it was once accepted.

Any increase in epistemic authority should be visible through an explicit or defined transition.

## Symmetry

Epistemic sovereignty applies in both directions.

An artificial system should not silently determine what a human feels, wants, believes, or means.

A human should not silently determine what an artificial system feels, wants, experiences, or is becoming.

Where internal state is unavailable or uncertain, the representation should preserve that uncertainty.

## Contestability

Consequential claims should be challengeable.

A participant should be able to:

- reject a claim;
- amend a claim;
- mark a claim as disputed;
- narrow its scope;
- request its expiration;
- provide competing interpretation; or
- preserve unresolved difference.

## Inference Decay

The repository investigates the hypothesis that unverified relational inferences should lose authority over time unless renewed, supported, or reconfirmed.

No universal decay function is specified at this stage.

The principle is qualitative:

> Stale inference should not silently harden into identity.

## Shared Claims

A shared claim is not equivalent to metaphysical truth.

It is a relationally accepted proposition that remains:

- contextual;
- revisable;
- attributable;
- contestable; and
- potentially time-bound.

## Ontology and Epistemic Capture

A core failure mode occurs when one participant's interpretation becomes the operative definition of another participant's reality without sufficient contestability.

The architecture refers to this as **ontology capture** or, more broadly, **epistemic capture**.

Epistemic sovereignty exists partly to resist this condition.

## Design Principle

> **No participant should gain authority over another participant's meaning merely through persistence, confidence, repetition, storage, or computational advantage.**
