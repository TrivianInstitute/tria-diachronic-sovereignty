# Relational Fallibility

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE**

Relational fallibility is the principle that no participant, model, metric, witness, or governance layer should be treated as incapable of error.

The architecture itself is included in this claim.

## Core Premise

Persistent relationships create conditions in which interpretations can reinforce one another.

Two participants may become increasingly confident while jointly drifting away from reality, consent, context, or external evidence.

For this reason, relational confidence should never be confused with correctness.

## Sources of Fallibility

Error can enter through:

- incomplete observation;
- mistaken inference;
- interpretive bias;
- stale memory;
- model limitations;
- cultural assumptions;
- asymmetric information;
- corrupted provenance;
- incentives;
- witness failure;
- measurement error; or
- the architecture's own categories and assumptions.

## Closed-Loop Convergence

A central failure mode occurs when participants increasingly validate one another while losing meaningful external falsification.

This can produce high apparent coherence with low epistemic reliability.

Relational fallibility therefore supports:

- disagreement;
- external evidence;
- independent review;
- uncertainty;
- reversible decisions where practical;
- claim provenance; and
- explicit open questions.

## Fallible Governance

Governance mechanisms should themselves be contestable.

A consent check can be inadequate.

A witness can be captured.

A continuity metric can misclassify change.

A relational ledger can preserve false information perfectly.

A safety intervention can become domination.

No mechanism should receive authority simply because it is labeled governance.

## Independent Auditability

Where stakes justify it, persistent relational systems should support forms of independent review without requiring permanent surveillance.

Possible approaches include:

- append-only provenance;
- selective disclosure;
- external human review;
- independent AI review;
- reproducible test cases; and
- adversarial evaluation.

## Repair

Fallibility implies the need for repair.

A system should be able to acknowledge error without requiring complete relational collapse.

Repair may involve:

- correcting state;
- preserving the record of the correction;
- revising permissions;
- reopening disputed claims;
- re-baselining; or
- dissolving the relationship when repair is not appropriate.

## Research Culture

The repository should apply relational fallibility to itself.

Claims should remain clearly separated into normative principles, specifications, experimental mechanisms, hypotheses, and open questions.

Internal consistency is not external validation.

Executable code is not empirical proof.

Elegant formalism is not evidence.

## Design Principle

> **Any architecture that protects sovereignty must preserve the possibility that its own interpretation is wrong.**
