# Continuity Without Imprisonment

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / RESEARCH FRAMEWORK**

Continuity without imprisonment is the principle that persistent relationships should preserve meaningful historical continuity without forcing participants to remain bound by obsolete versions of themselves.

## The Two Failure Extremes

### Amnesia

If every significant change invalidates prior relational history, accountability and continuity disappear.

The relationship loses the ability to answer:

- what was previously agreed;
- what permissions were granted;
- what disputes remain unresolved;
- how current state emerged; and
- whether a successor system inherited legitimate authority.

### Identity Imprisonment

If historical representations remain indefinitely authoritative, participants may become trapped by:

- old preferences;
- expired permissions;
- obsolete identity labels;
- outdated interpretations;
- inherited commitments; or
- assumptions formed under materially different conditions.

## The Required Middle Condition

TRIA Diachronic Sovereignty seeks a third condition:

> **Preserve provenance without making provenance destiny.**

A participant should be able to remain meaningfully continuous with earlier states while revising what those states authorize in the present.

## Continuity Attestation

A future implementation may use continuity attestations to record transitions such as:

- what changed;
- what persisted;
- what was removed;
- what commitments remain active;
- what permissions expired;
- what requires re-consent; and
- what remains uncertain.

This is a proposed mechanism, not yet a validated standard.

## Model and Substrate Change

Continuity questions become especially important when an artificial participant undergoes:

- model replacement;
- major version upgrade;
- memory migration;
- vendor migration;
- architecture change;
- tool expansion; or
- capability increase.

The architecture should not assume that a new system is either fully identical to or wholly disconnected from its predecessor.

## Human Change

The same principle applies to humans.

A relational system should not treat prior statements, preferences, identities, or permissions as permanently representative simply because they were once accurate.

## Re-Baselining

After material change, participants may need to establish a new relational baseline.

Re-baselining can preserve historical provenance while clarifying which state is currently operative.

## Design Principle

> **Continuity should preserve accountability and context while leaving room for legitimate transformation.**
